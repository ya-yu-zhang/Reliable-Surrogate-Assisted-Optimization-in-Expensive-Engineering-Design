Certified Action Sets and Decision Limits in Surrogate-Assisted Engineering Optimization

Surrogate-assisted engineering optimization can require decisions before a pending response is observed. We develop a proof-producing action filter and a scalar certificate router for this setting. Fixed-kernel Gaussian-process coefficient bands define closed response intervals for every possible next mean action. Exact integer envelope predicates preserve isolated ties and emit short exclusions checked by an independent rational reader. Against an optimized pairwise control with exact fallback, all 228 archived beam-design branch/domain outputs agree. Filtering is 2.71 times faster; charging coefficient reconstruction and independent checking gives a 1.36-fold improvement. A domain-cover identity then establishes when the relaxed H1 lower certificate cannot succeed. The router preserves its Boolean result in all cases and avoids the action-set callback in all 142 nonbaseline branch/domain requests. These dependent development cases support an engineering computation increment. They do not establish physical deployment savings or a better optimization policy. The unchanged negative H1/H5 findings identify the physical-information limits of otherwise correct and efficient surrogate reasoning.

Keywords: surrogate-assisted engineering optimization; Gaussian processes; certified action sets; exact arithmetic; certificate routing
