Certified Action Sets and Decision Limits in Surrogate-Assisted Engineering Optimization

A useful surrogate must support a credible evaluation decision, not only accurate predictions. We develop a conditional audit that distinguishes action changes, finite-horizon consequences and information costs. For a fixed-kernel Gaussian process with one unknown nonnegative response, enclosed affine posterior means define a response-domain outer set of possible next actions. An exact rational envelope computes the complete relaxed-band set in O(M log M) arithmetic operations, retains isolated ties and supplies at most two rival indices per exclusion. On 114 archived engineering branches, both declared response domains retain 2-7 of 1024 actions and include every stored next action. All 232,564 original exclusion proofs and binary coefficient systems are reverified. The envelope produces identical action sets with 3.19-fold faster set construction, or 2.62-fold including separate certificate replay, relative to the frozen pairwise implementation. These are computational-stage improvements. The engineering bounds remain too weak to certify any of 71 nonbaseline H1 contrasts; H5 adaptive allocation also gives no independent diagnostic advantage. Full deployment savings and physical accuracy of the decision margin remain unestablished. The study contributes an executable, proof-producing action-set operator and an engineering evaluation separating computational efficiency from decision sufficiency.

Keywords: expensive engineering optimization; Gaussian processes; numerical certificates; affine envelopes; decision auditing
