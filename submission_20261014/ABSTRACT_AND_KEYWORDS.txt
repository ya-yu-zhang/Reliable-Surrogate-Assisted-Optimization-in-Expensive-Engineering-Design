Local Response Stability for Certified Two-Step Rollout in Engineering Optimization

Two-step rollout can require a complete response-dependent continuation partition for every first action. We formulate a finite-menu solver that first certifies a reference continuation on a local response domain and bounds the omitted adaptation by its complement probability. With a positive-response planning model, a bounded terminal reward links this domain certificate to first-action value intervals. Residual enclosures, conditional-reward curvature and directed integration support a checked selection rule at a declared tolerance, with complete-projection fallback and unresolved outcomes. A historical aggregate on 43 archived beam-design states reports complete projections reduced from 114 to 25, with only a 1.029-fold checked-stage timing improvement. Paid engineering replay has mixed outcomes. The executed positive-scope source and per-state records are currently unavailable, so these empirical reports await verification. The mathematical contribution is the local-reduction and acceptance mechanism for an explicit finite binary model; engineering-policy superiority and complete reproducibility remain unestablished.

Keywords: surrogate-assisted engineering optimization; two-step rollout; Gaussian processes; response stability; validated numerical integration
