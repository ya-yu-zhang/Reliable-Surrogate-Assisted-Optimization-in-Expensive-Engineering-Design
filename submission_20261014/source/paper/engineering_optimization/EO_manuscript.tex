\begin{abstract}
Two-step rollout can require a complete response-dependent continuation partition for every first action. We formulate a finite-menu solver that first certifies a reference continuation on a local response domain and bounds the omitted adaptation by its complement probability. With a positive-response planning model, a bounded terminal reward links this domain certificate to first-action value intervals. Residual enclosures, conditional-reward curvature and directed integration support a checked selection rule at a declared tolerance, with complete-projection fallback and unresolved outcomes. A historical aggregate on 43 archived beam-design states reports complete projections reduced from 114 to 25, with only a 1.029-fold checked-stage timing improvement. Paid engineering replay has mixed outcomes. The executed positive-scope source and per-state records are currently unavailable, so these empirical reports await verification. The mathematical contribution is the local-reduction and acceptance mechanism for an explicit finite binary model; engineering-policy superiority and complete reproducibility remain unestablished.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Surrogate-assisted engineering optimization spends expensive evaluations according to predictions and a model-management rule \citep{ref01,ref02,ref03,ref35}. A two-step lookahead must also account for how the next action will change after observing the pending response. Computing a complete response-dependent action partition for every possible first action can be unnecessary when a continuation is stable over most of the planning probability mass.

We study first-action optimization on an existing finite menu with a fixed posterior-mean continuation. The proposed solver first establishes a response region where a reference continuation is certain, integrates its fixed-action terminal value, and bounds the omitted adaptation by the probability of leaving that region. It constructs a complete response projection only on branches where this local bound cannot resolve the decision. Directed integration and posterior arithmetic enclosures support a numerical selection tolerance, with explicit unresolved outcomes when the assumptions or resource budget are insufficient.

The resulting output is an accepted first-action selection for a declared rollout objective. This differs from the earlier project output, which was a set of possible next actions without a numerical rollout value certificate. The contribution under consideration is the integrated local-reduction and checked-selection mechanism. Affine envelopes, bounded-reward coupling, interval selection and residual estimates are established ingredients; they are not presented individually as new optimization principles.

An engineering use case is a positive compliance objective with a small prescribed first-action menu. A log-response GP supplies a positive planning distribution while the existing raw-response mean rule remains the continuation. This distinction fixes the mathematical problem and avoids silently changing the base policy. It does not establish that the new planning distribution is better calibrated.

The development record concerns 43 nontrivial states from one beam-design family. Its positive-response source and per-state execution files are currently unavailable, although an aggregate was preserved remotely. We therefore distinguish the current mathematical specification, historical reported numerical observations, and engineering-policy evidence throughout. The raw-Gaussian negative result and preceding action-set study are preserved, rather than replaced by a positive-model interpretation. This draft does not claim a complete reproducibility release.

\section{Relationship to existing lookahead methods}

Two-step lookahead and rollout are established acquisition approaches \citep{ref39}. Lee et al.\ reduce rollout computation through quasi-Monte Carlo, common random numbers and control variates, and also investigate policy search \citep{ref43}. Our calculation instead fixes a finite first-action menu and base continuation, then asks where a complete response partition can be avoided while retaining a numerical selection bound. No superiority over those broader acquisition optimizers is inferred.

Ling et al.\ develop an epsilon-optimal GP planning framework with Lipschitz rewards and an anytime branch-and-bound variant \citep{ref44}. Thus approximate-optimal GP planning itself is not the novelty claimed here. The present scope is a particular two-evaluation terminal objective, positive log-response model, affine raw-response continuation and finite binary-system error contract. A guarantee for this subproblem does not establish global or Bayes-optimal search.

Affine posterior updates and breakpoint envelopes also occur in knowledge-gradient computation \citep{ref40,ref42}. Our earlier band-feasibility operator adapts that geometry to closed response intervals and independently replayable exclusions, using exact predicates \citep{ref41}. It serves as the complete-projection fallback. The local route does not require all those action domains initially.

Log-objective expected improvement is established \citep{ref45}; MPFR provides a correctly rounded arithmetic foundation \citep{ref46}. Neither positivity nor use of interval arithmetic alone constitutes the proposed increment. The concrete question is whether a response-stability certificate can guide a checked rollout solver to avoid unnecessary complete projections. The direct same-guarantee route is the relevant control for that question. Engineering-policy efficacy remains a separate outcome.

\section{Discussion and limitations}

The mechanism links a policy property to the computation needed for first-action optimization: when one continuation is certified on a high-probability response region, a bounded reward controls the cost of ignoring adaptation outside that region. Competitive branches receive more detailed analysis only when this bound is insufficient. The mathematical guarantee is conditional on valid model and arithmetic enclosures, and the algorithm may return unresolved. Establishing equivalence to the historical implementation requires recovery of that implementation.

The engineering scope is specific. Compliance is positive, the first-action menu and original mean continuation are fixed, and the posterior parameters are not refitted during planning. The log-response planning distribution and raw-response continuation represent different declared objects. Kernel-function approximation, posterior calibration, physical/PDE accuracy and continuous-domain optimizer convergence are not covered by the numerical theorem. A tolerance relative to the recorded incumbent is not an engineering accuracy certificate.

The historical summary suggests that avoiding many complete projections does not necessarily produce a large checked-stage timing gain: refinement and verification remain costs. Its 1.029 ratio is too limited to support a general acceleration claim. Preparation and historical input acquisition remain outside that ratio. The mixed paid outcomes likewise provide no evidence of a uniformly better engineering policy.

The most immediate empirical limitation is availability, not experiment count. Original positive-scope code, decisions, certificates and timing rows are needed to audit the reported comparison. Additional experiments would not recover those records or verify the historical execution. No new experiments are proposed in this revision. Existing necessary checks and unfavorable findings remain part of the evidence.

The complete action-set manuscript immediately preceding this revision is retained byte for byte in the archive. The supplement retains its strong same-output comparison, nominal-band diagnostic, H1 obstruction and all earlier adverse tables. Three of 25 supporting paired-grid raw records also remain unavailable; their derived tables do not establish mesh convergence or physical accuracy at the decision margin. These older evidence gaps remain distinct from the positive-scope execution gap.

\section{Conclusion}

A response-stability certificate can bound the value error of replacing an adaptive second action by a fixed reference action. Combined with posterior enclosures, validated conditional integration, selective complete projection and a checked acceptance rule, it yields a finite-menu rollout selection mechanism for a declared positive-response model. The historical beam aggregate reports fewer complete projections, a small checked-stage timing improvement and mixed engineering outcomes. Missing execution files currently prevent independent verification of those reports and a submission-ready claim. The current contribution is a mathematically specified candidate solver with an explicit evidence boundary; engineering-policy superiority remains unestablished.
