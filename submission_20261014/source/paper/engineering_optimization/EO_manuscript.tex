\begin{abstract}
Two-step rollout selects a first engineering design by anticipating a response-dependent continuation. A finite-menu algorithm is developed in which response-stability regions are certified and conditional posteriors are constructed for encountered actions. Regional terminal-reward bounds enclose the contribution of uncomputed regions and certify first-action selection to a prescribed model-value tolerance. The certificate covers positive completions of uncomputed conditional laws, and a piecewise error bound separates policy switches from smooth integration. Code and clean training inputs were frozen before one paired evaluation on 43 previously observed beam-design states. Both regional and uniform-reward routes certify all states without complete continuation projection. Regional bounds reduce cell operations from 640 to 585 and conditional posterior constructions from 161 to 157. Checked execution takes 12.23 and 10.73 seconds, respectively, plus shared preparation. Certified decision computation is established for a finite structural-design problem; previously paid physical outcomes remain mixed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Surrogate-assisted engineering optimization allocates expensive evaluations using predictive models and a model-management rule \citep{ref01,ref02,ref03,ref35}. Two-step planning accounts for how the next design choice changes with the pending response. Computing a complete response-dependent continuation partition can resolve action identities that have little influence on the current first-action choice.

First-action selection is certified by discovering local response-stability regions and computing conditional posteriors for encountered continuations. The terminal reward is integrated over each certified region. The contribution of unassigned regions is enclosed by a positive-response bound, and further region discovery or integral refinement is directed by the current value uncertainty. The first action is accepted when a checked inequality places its model-value regret within the prescribed tolerance.

The method combines a regional terminal-reward enclosure, an adaptive region-discovery and conditional-query algorithm, and a finite-menu selection certificate valid for every positive completion of uncomputed conditional laws. Unresolved continuation value is included in the stopping rule, allowing the first-action comparison to be certified before the continuation partition is complete. A piecewise error bound separates unresolved switches from smooth integration. The continuation policy is the existing affine raw-response posterior mean; a log-response planning model supplies positive terminal responses. This two-model specification retains the prescribed continuation while bounding its planning reward.

The implementation starts from clean training snapshots for 43 archived beam-design states. One frozen paired comparison changes the unknown-region reward interval while retaining the discovery algorithm, posterior arithmetic and checking procedure. It measures how regional value information changes the required computations. Previously paid two-evaluation outcomes provide the corresponding engineering comparison. The supplement preserves the proofs and earlier computational and engineering studies.

\section{Relationship to existing lookahead methods}

\section{Discussion}

Local response stability connects action identity to the value of additional computation. A certified region makes one conditional response relevant; the remaining-reward bound quantifies the value uncertainty elsewhere. When the first response is already small, its capped value limits the contribution of every continuation. The algorithm uses these two forms of information to certify the supplied first-action comparison with an incomplete continuation partition.

The paired control isolates the contribution of the regional reward interval. Both routes avoid complete projection, while the regional bound reduces cell operations and posterior requests. Its additional bound calculations accompany a longer checked duration in the recorded pass. Posterior requests also repeat training solves and validation, which remain included in the reported cost. The result is a reduction in computational requests with a measured time increase.

The guarantee concerns finite-menu model-value regret under the positive conditional-response contract. It encloses arithmetic for the declared binary matrices and queried marginals; kernel-function approximation, an unqueried full joint covariance, statistical calibration and physical/PDE error require separate analysis. Fixed parameter bands, finite precision and resource caps can leave a state unresolved. The engineering evaluation uses one fixed-volume beam family and previously observed states. Its mixed paid outcomes support the stated decision-computation application, while broader design-quality, physical-budget and continuous-domain conclusions remain untested.

The supplement separates the earlier action-set, full-projection and certificate-transformation computations. Their costs and numerical results belong to their respective algorithms. The availability statement identifies the missing historical raw records.

\section{Conclusion}

Response-stability-guided regional value enclosures yield a finite-menu rollout algorithm with on-demand conditional-posterior construction. The algorithm certifies first-action selection for every admissible positive completion of uncomputed conditional laws, and its error analysis treats policy switches separately from smooth integration. Both routes certify all 43 beam-design states without complete continuation projection. Regional bounds reduce cell operations from 640 to 585 and conditional posterior constructions from 161 to 157; their checked stage takes 12.23 s compared with 10.73 s for the uniform control. These findings establish a certified computational method for the specified two-evaluation engineering decision.
