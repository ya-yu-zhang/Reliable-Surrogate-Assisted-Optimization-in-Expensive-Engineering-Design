\begin{abstract}
Two-step rollout can require a complete response-dependent continuation partition for every first action. We propose a finite-menu solver that certifies a reference continuation on a response domain and bounds omitted adaptation by its complement probability. A positive-response planning model makes terminal improvement bounded. Posterior residual enclosures, conditional-reward curvature and directed integration produce a checked first-action selection rule, with selective complete projection and unresolved outcomes. A prospectively frozen implementation verifies both local and direct routes on all 43 archived beam-design states at tolerance m0/1000000, with identical selections. Local stability reduces complete projections from 114 to 25, but its checked numerical stage takes 5.47 seconds versus 4.93 seconds for the direct route. Paid engineering replay has mixed outcomes. Source, fixed inputs and per-state records are preserved. The contribution is a response-stability mechanism for certified finite-menu rollout optimization; the evidence establishes model-based numerical applicability, without engineering-policy superiority or deployment savings.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Surrogate-assisted engineering optimization spends expensive evaluations according to predictions and a model-management rule \citep{ref01,ref02,ref03,ref35}. A two-step lookahead must also account for how the next action will change after observing the pending response. Computing a complete response-dependent action partition for every possible first action can be unnecessary when a continuation is stable over most of the planning probability mass.

We study first-action optimization on an existing finite menu with a fixed posterior-mean continuation. The proposed solver first establishes a response region where a reference continuation is certain, integrates its fixed-action terminal value, and bounds the omitted adaptation by the probability of leaving that region. It constructs a complete response projection only on branches where this local bound cannot resolve the decision. Directed integration and posterior arithmetic enclosures support a numerical selection tolerance, with explicit unresolved outcomes when the assumptions or resource budget are insufficient.

The resulting output is an accepted first-action selection for a declared rollout objective. This differs from the earlier project output, which was a set of possible next actions without a numerical rollout value certificate. The contribution under consideration is the integrated local-reduction and checked-selection mechanism. Affine envelopes, bounded-reward coupling, interval selection and residual estimates are established ingredients; they are not presented individually as new optimization principles.

An engineering use case is a positive compliance objective with a small prescribed first-action menu. A log-response GP supplies a positive planning distribution while the existing raw-response mean rule remains the continuation. This distinction fixes the mathematical problem and avoids silently changing the base policy. It does not establish that the new planning distribution is better calibrated.

The prospectively frozen v2 implementation is evaluated on all 43 nontrivial archived states from one beam-design family. Both it and the direct same-guarantee route return checked selections on every state. Source, fixed-input snapshots and per-state records are preserved. The local route avoids most complete projections but is slower in the recorded single pass, and the paid engineering outcomes remain mixed. The raw-Gaussian negative result and preceding action-set study are retained as separate evidence.

\section{Relationship to existing lookahead methods}

Two-step lookahead and rollout are established acquisition approaches \citep{ref39}. Lee et al.\ reduce rollout computation through quasi-Monte Carlo, common random numbers and control variates, and also investigate policy search \citep{ref43}. Finite menus also appear in their policy-search formulation. Our additional computational object is a certified response-stability domain and its deterministic value-error bound for a fixed continuation, combined with posterior arithmetic and checked integration. No superiority over their acquisition optimizers is inferred.

Ling et al.\ develop an epsilon-optimal GP planning framework with Lipschitz rewards and an anytime branch-and-bound variant \citep{ref44}. Thus approximate-optimal GP planning itself is not the novelty claimed here. The present scope is a particular two-evaluation terminal objective, positive log-response model, affine raw-response continuation and finite binary-system error contract. A guarantee for this subproblem does not establish global or Bayes-optimal search. The event-error bound needs bounded rewards and certified policy identity on a response domain, rather than a global Lipschitz constant for the composed fixed-policy value. Curvature is used only on identified smooth action/incumbent pieces.

Affine posterior updates and breakpoint envelopes also occur in knowledge-gradient computation \citep{ref40,ref42}. Our earlier band-feasibility operator adapts that geometry to closed response intervals and independently replayable exclusions, using exact predicates \citep{ref41}. It serves as the complete-projection fallback. The local route does not require all those action domains initially.

Log-objective expected improvement is established \citep{ref45}; MPFR provides a correctly rounded arithmetic foundation \citep{ref46}. Neither positivity nor use of interval arithmetic alone constitutes the proposed increment. The concrete question is whether a response-stability certificate can guide a checked rollout solver to avoid unnecessary complete projections. The direct same-guarantee route is the relevant control for that question. Engineering-policy efficacy remains a separate outcome.

\section{Discussion and limitations}

The mechanism links a policy property to the computation needed for first-action optimization: when one continuation is certified on a high-probability response region, a bounded reward controls the cost of ignoring adaptation outside that region. Competitive branches receive more detailed analysis only when this bound is insufficient. The mathematical guarantee is conditional on valid model and arithmetic enclosures, and the algorithm may return unresolved. The v2 implementation supplies executable evidence for this specified mechanism; it makes no equivalence claim about the unavailable historical program.

The engineering scope is specific. Compliance is positive, the first-action menu and original mean continuation are fixed, and the posterior parameters are not refitted during planning. The log-response planning distribution and raw-response continuation represent different declared objects. Kernel-function approximation, posterior calibration, physical/PDE accuracy and continuous-domain optimizer convergence are not covered by the numerical theorem. A tolerance relative to the recorded incumbent is not an engineering accuracy certificate.

The frozen v2 comparison shows that avoiding complete projections need not reduce total numerical time. Local dominance, refinement and verification remain costs: the local checked stage takes 5.47 s versus 4.93 s for the direct route. Both require the same 3.62 s preparation. These single-pass results support no time-saving conclusion. The mixed paid outcomes likewise provide no evidence of a uniformly better engineering policy.

The v2 numerical evidence is now available as a separate complete record. The missing historical positive-scope files remain missing, and the new run does not verify their timing or source hashes. It uses the same previously observed states, not independent validation. One beam family, one paired timing pass and dependent paid replay limit the empirical reach. No experimental expansion is used to change these conclusions.

The preceding complete action-set manuscript is retained byte for byte in the archive. The supplement retains its strong same-output comparison, nominal-band diagnostic, H1 obstruction and all earlier adverse tables. Three of 25 supporting paired-grid raw records also remain unavailable; their derived tables do not establish mesh convergence or physical accuracy at the decision margin. These older evidence gaps remain distinct from the positive-scope execution gap.

\section{Conclusion}

A response-stability certificate bounds the value error of replacing an adaptive second action by a fixed reference action. Posterior enclosures, validated conditional integration, selective complete projection and a checked acceptance rule turn it into an executable finite-menu rollout solver for an explicit positive-response model. The frozen v2 run returns matching checked choices on all 43 engineering states and reduces complete projections from 114 to 25. It shows no numerical time advantage, and paid outcomes do not establish engineering-policy superiority. The contribution is reliable solution of a declared engineering optimization subproblem; statistical calibration, physical accuracy, continuous-domain convergence and deployment savings remain outside the evidence.
