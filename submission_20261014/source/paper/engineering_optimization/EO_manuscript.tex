\begin{abstract}
Two-step rollout couples first-action selection to response-dependent continuation. We propose a finite-menu algorithm that discovers certified stability regions and requests conditional posterior calculations only for encountered actions. Regional terminal-reward enclosures certify selection without a complete continuation partition. The guarantee covers every positive completion of uncomputed conditional responses, including discontinuous fixed-policy rewards. Code and clean training inputs were frozen before one paired evaluation on 43 archived beam-design states. Both regional and uniform-reward routes certify all states, matching earlier selections without reading historical action domains. Regional bounds reduce cell operations from 640 to 585 and conditional posterior constructions from 161 to 157. Their checked stage takes 12.23 seconds versus 10.73 seconds, with shared preparation charged separately. Existing engineering outcomes remain mixed. The contribution is a response-stability-guided algorithm for certified rollout selection under a declared conditional model. Numerical applicability is demonstrated; engineering-policy superiority, acceleration and continuous-domain convergence remain unestablished.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Surrogate-assisted engineering optimization spends expensive evaluations according to predictions and a model-management rule \citep{ref01,ref02,ref03,ref35}. Two-step planning must account for how the next design choice changes with the pending response. A complete response-dependent continuation partition can contain detail that is irrelevant to the current first-action decision.

We study a fixed first-action menu and an affine raw-response posterior-mean continuation. The proposed algorithm discovers local response regions in which a continuation is certified, encloses its positive terminal reward, and requests conditional posterior calculations only for encountered actions. An unassigned region retains a bounded continuation remainder. Its value uncertainty determines whether to discover another region or refine an existing integral. A checked decision inequality can certify the first action while some response identities remain uncomputed.

The methodological contribution has three connected parts: a regional terminal-reward enclosure; a lazy region-discovery and conditional-query algorithm with a positive-completion selection guarantee; and a finite-state implementation tested from clean engineering training inputs. A local error bound explains how unresolved switch cells and smooth integration contribute separately, without assuming a globally Lipschitz policy-composed value. Classical rollout, EI decomposition, affine dominance and interval arithmetic are acknowledged ingredients. Their individual use is not the novelty claimed here.

A positive log-response planning model and the existing raw-response mean continuation define different declared objects. This makes a positive compliance objective suitable for the stated terminal-reward range while retaining the original base policy. It does not establish improved statistical calibration. The lazy certificate validates queried bivariate marginals and bounds uncomputed positive conditional laws; it does not certify an unqueried full joint covariance or physical error.

Code and clean inputs were frozen before a single paired run on all 43 nontrivial archived beam-design states. Both the regional route and its uniform-reward control certify every state without historical action domains, old posteriors or integration certificates. Regional bounds reduce cell operations from 640 to 585 and conditional posterior constructions from 161 to 157, but the checked stage is slower. Existing engineering outcomes remain mixed. Earlier v2, certificate-transformation and adverse records are retained as supporting evidence; all states have already been observed in development.

\section{Relationship to existing lookahead methods}

Two-step optimization, rollout and approximate-optimal GP planning are established. Wu and Frazier optimize a two-step acquisition using gradient estimation and variance reduction, including one-dimensional Gauss--Hermite integration in the sequential setting \citep{ref39}. Lee et al.\ combine quasi-Monte Carlo, common random numbers and control variates, and also investigate finite policy search \citep{ref43}. Ling et al.\ provide epsilon-optimal GP planning with Lipschitz rewards and an anytime branch-and-bound variant \citep{ref44}. These works already establish the broader motivation and several computational tools used here.

Affine mean updates and breakpoint envelopes occur in knowledge-gradient computation \citep{ref40,ref42}, and exact predicates are classical numerical tools \citep{ref41}. Expected-improvement decomposition and log-objective moments are also established \citep{ref45}. The proposed increment is the algorithm that combines a computable response-stability domain with a remaining-reward range to schedule region discovery and conditional-posterior requests, then certifies a first action before every continuation identity is known. The elementary range identity is not claimed as an independent optimization principle.

The numerical control uses the same local-discovery algorithm and conditional-model contract, changing only the unknown-cell reward interval. It tests the specific role of that interval rather than comparing different acquisition objectives or outputs. Prior works supply the novelty context; no claim of being the first two-step, finite-menu or epsilon-guaranteed GP method is made.

\section{Discussion and limitations}

The algorithm's contribution is a numerical solution method for the declared two-evaluation design-selection problem. It uses certified action identity and remaining-value uncertainty to decide which conditional computation is relevant to the current first choice. Its guarantee applies across policy switches and to positive completions of uncomputed conditional laws; complete global continuation projection is not an input requirement.

The paired control separates shared algorithm structure from the regional bound. Both routes avoid complete projection; the regional interval additionally reduces cell operations and conditional-posterior requests. The gain is modest and the regional route is slower in the recorded pass. Repeated training solves and validation inside posterior requests are included in that cost. Neither a universal complexity advantage nor numerical acceleration is established.

Applicability is demonstrated on one positive-compliance beam family, using fixed parameters, supplied first menus and an unchanged mean continuation. All states were previously observed. The corresponding paid outcomes remain mixed, so the evidence does not establish better engineering designs, physical evaluation savings or independent closed-loop efficacy. The certificate concerns finite binary-system model arithmetic; calibration, kernel-function approximation, physical/PDE accuracy and continuous-domain convergence are separate questions. The unqueried full joint covariance is not certified.

Earlier action-set, full-projection and certificate-transformation studies are retained in the supplement. Their projection counts, timing ratios and width reductions concern other computations and are not combined with the final algorithm's results. Missing historical raw files are disclosed in the availability statement. Additional datasets or repeated timing are not used to turn these limited findings into a stronger efficacy claim.

\section{Conclusion}

Response-stability-guided regional value enclosures support a lazy finite-menu rollout algorithm that requests conditional posteriors only for encountered continuations. Its selection certificate covers every positive completion of uncomputed conditional laws; a local error bound handles switching cells separately from smooth integration. Starting from cleaned training snapshots, both regional and uniform-reward routes certify all 43 engineering states without complete projection. Regional bounds use 585 rather than 640 cell operations and 157 rather than 161 conditional posterior constructions, but take more checked time. The contribution is certified computation of the declared engineering optimization subproblem. Mixed physical outcomes, observed-data reuse and the finite conditional-model contract limit stronger efficacy or generality claims.
