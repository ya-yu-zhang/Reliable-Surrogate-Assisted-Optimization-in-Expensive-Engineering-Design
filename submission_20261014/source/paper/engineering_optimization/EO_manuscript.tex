\begin{abstract}
Two-step rollout couples first-action choice to response-dependent continuation. We propose response-stability-guided value enclosures for a finite-menu engineering optimization problem. Decomposing positive-response terminal improvement into a first-evaluation baseline and bounded continuation remainder yields regional selection certificates without identifying every continuation action. Directed posterior and integration bounds control numerical error. A frozen probability-bound implementation verifies matching selections on 43 archived beam-design states. Local stability reduces complete projections from 114 to 25, but takes 5.47 seconds versus 4.93 seconds directly. Subsequent regional postprocessing tightens all 89 fixed-continuation intervals by a median 37.1\%, without model reconstruction or new conditional-reward integration. Choices remain unchanged. Paid engineering replay is mixed. The contribution is a regional value-enclosure mechanism for certified rollout selection under a fixed finite model. Numerical applicability is demonstrated; time savings, engineering-policy superiority and continuous-domain convergence are unestablished.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Surrogate-assisted engineering optimization spends expensive evaluations according to predictions and a model-management rule \citep{ref01,ref02,ref03,ref35}. A two-step lookahead must also account for how the next action will change after observing the pending response. Computing a complete response-dependent action partition for every possible first action can be unnecessary when a continuation is stable over most of the planning probability mass.

We study first-action optimization on an existing finite menu with a fixed posterior-mean continuation. A certified response-stability region identifies where one reference continuation is certain. Positivity decomposes terminal improvement into an already obtained first-evaluation baseline and a remaining adaptation value bounded by the capped first response. Integrating the known part on the stable region and enclosing the unknown part gives a regional first-action value certificate. A decision-gap condition can certify a first action without a complete continuation partition. Directed posterior and integration enclosures support the numerical tolerance, with explicit unresolved outcomes.

The proposed contribution is a response-stability-guided regional value-enclosure method for the declared rollout subproblem. It combines a computable affine stability region, a terminal-reward range and a finite-menu decision certificate, including stored-leaf conversion without new conditional quadrature. The earlier project returned possible next-action sets; that internal change of output does not establish originality against the literature. Affine envelopes, expected-improvement decomposition, bounded-reward arguments, interval selection and residual estimates are established ingredients.

An engineering use case is a positive compliance objective with a small prescribed first-action menu. A log-response GP supplies a positive planning distribution while the existing raw-response mean rule remains the continuation. This distinction fixes the mathematical problem and avoids silently changing the base policy. It does not establish that the new planning distribution is better calibrated.

The prospectively frozen v2 implementation is evaluated on all 43 nontrivial archived states from one beam-design family. Both it and the direct same-guarantee route return checked selections on every state. Source, fixed-input snapshots and per-state records are preserved. The local route avoids most complete projections but is slower in the recorded single pass, and the paid engineering outcomes remain mixed. The raw-Gaussian negative result and preceding action-set study are retained as separate evidence. A subsequent, explicitly post hoc regional conversion reuses its 89 checked fixed-continuation branch certificates. All are narrowed, with a median width reduction of 37.1\%; 86 also narrow beyond a simple first-step baseline constraint. This later conversion changes neither the frozen choices nor their original projection and timing records.

\section{Relationship to existing lookahead methods}

Wu and Frazier develop practical two-step acquisition optimization, including one-dimensional Gauss--Hermite quadrature in the sequential setting \citep{ref39}. Two-step lookahead and one-dimensional conditioning are therefore established capabilities. Lee et al.\ reduce rollout computation through quasi-Monte Carlo, common random numbers and control variates, and also investigate policy search \citep{ref43}. Finite menus also appear in their policy-search formulation. Our additional computational object is a certified response-stability domain and its deterministic value-error bound for a fixed continuation, combined with posterior arithmetic and checked integration. The first-step baseline decomposition is also related to their use of EI as a known-mean control variate; the decomposition alone is not claimed as new. Here the stable-region remainder supplies a deterministic value range, rather than a variance-reduction result. No superiority over their acquisition optimizers is inferred.

Ling et al.\ develop an epsilon-optimal GP planning framework with Lipschitz rewards and an anytime branch-and-bound variant \citep{ref44}. Thus approximate-optimal GP planning itself is not the novelty claimed here. The present scope is a particular two-evaluation terminal objective, positive log-response model, affine raw-response continuation and finite binary-system error contract. A guarantee for this subproblem does not establish global or Bayes-optimal search. The event-error bound needs bounded rewards and certified policy identity on a response domain, rather than a global Lipschitz constant for the composed fixed-policy value. Curvature is used only on identified smooth action/incumbent pieces.

Affine posterior updates and breakpoint envelopes also occur in knowledge-gradient computation \citep{ref40,ref42}. Our earlier band-feasibility operator adapts that geometry to closed response intervals and independently replayable exclusions, using exact predicates \citep{ref41}. It serves as the complete-projection fallback. The local route does not require all those action domains initially.

Log-objective expected improvement is established \citep{ref45}; MPFR provides a correctly rounded arithmetic foundation \citep{ref46}. Neither positivity nor use of interval arithmetic alone constitutes the proposed increment. The concrete question is whether a response-stability certificate can guide a checked rollout solver to avoid unnecessary complete projections. The direct same-guarantee route is the relevant control for that question. Engineering-policy efficacy remains a separate outcome.

\section{Discussion and limitations}

The mechanism links a policy property to decision-relevant value uncertainty. Regional enclosures retain the first-evaluation baseline and the known continuation remainder, while bounding the unidentified remainder by its capped first-response moment. The selection inequality can resolve a first action without resolving every future identity. A nondegenerate example shows that the policy-composed reward may be discontinuous even with smooth affine means and Gaussian log responses. The range argument remains valid there. Its finite-model guarantee is conditional on valid arithmetic enclosures, and resource limits can leave a decision unresolved.

The engineering scope is specific. Compliance is positive, the first-action menu and original mean continuation are fixed, and the posterior parameters are not refitted during planning. The log-response planning distribution and raw-response continuation represent different declared objects. Kernel-function approximation, posterior calibration, physical/PDE accuracy and continuous-domain optimizer convergence are not covered by the numerical theorem. A tolerance relative to the recorded incumbent is not an engineering accuracy certificate.

The frozen v2 comparison shows that avoiding complete projections need not reduce total numerical time. Local dominance, refinement and verification remain costs: the local checked stage takes 5.47 s versus 4.93 s for the direct route. Both require the same 3.62 s preparation. These single-pass results support no time-saving conclusion. The mixed paid outcomes likewise provide no evidence of a uniformly better engineering policy.

The regional result is a later certificate transformation, not a new end-to-end solver comparison. It tightens the stored value intervals, and the exact-endpoint attribution distinguishes its effect from the simple baseline lower bound. The v2 preparation already used archived exact domains to select its finite target list. Thus the recorded projection reduction concerns reconstruction after shared preparation; it does not establish from-scratch savings in identifying all continuation actions. No smaller projection count or improved engineering decision is inferred from postprocessing.

The v2 numerical evidence is now available as a separate complete record. The missing historical positive-scope files remain missing, and the new run does not verify their timing or source hashes. It uses the same previously observed states, not independent validation. One beam family, one paired timing pass and dependent paid replay limit the empirical reach. No experimental expansion is used to change these conclusions.

The preceding complete action-set manuscript is retained byte for byte in the archive. The supplement retains its strong same-output comparison, nominal-band diagnostic, H1 obstruction and all earlier adverse tables. Three of 25 supporting paired-grid raw records also remain unavailable; their derived tables do not establish mesh convergence or physical accuracy at the decision margin. These older evidence gaps remain distinct from the positive-scope execution gap.

\section{Conclusion}

Regional terminal-reward enclosures convert certified continuation stability into a first-action value range. Their unknown width is controlled by the capped first-response moment, and a decision-gap condition can certify a selection without a complete response partition. Stored-leaf conversion implements this mechanism without new conditional-reward quadrature. It narrows all 89 eligible archived branch intervals by a median 37.1\%, with additional narrowing beyond the first-step baseline in 86 branches. The precursor v2 run verifies matching selections on 43 engineering states but provides no time advantage, and paid outcomes remain mixed. The contribution concerns certified numerical optimization of the declared finite-menu subproblem. Statistical calibration, physical accuracy, superior engineering policies, continuous-domain convergence and deployment savings remain unestablished.
