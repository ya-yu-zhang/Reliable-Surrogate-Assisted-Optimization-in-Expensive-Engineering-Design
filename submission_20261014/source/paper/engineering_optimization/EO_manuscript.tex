\begin{abstract}
Two-step rollout couples first-action selection to response-dependent continuation. We propose a finite-menu algorithm that discovers certified stability regions and requests conditional posterior calculations only for encountered actions. Regional terminal-reward enclosures certify selection without a complete continuation partition. The guarantee covers every positive completion of uncomputed conditional responses, including discontinuous fixed-policy rewards. Code and clean training inputs were frozen before one paired evaluation on 43 archived beam-design states. Both regional and uniform-reward routes certify all states, matching earlier selections without reading historical action domains. Regional bounds reduce cell operations from 640 to 585 and conditional posterior constructions from 161 to 157. Their checked stage takes 12.23 seconds versus 10.73 seconds, with shared preparation charged separately. Existing engineering outcomes remain mixed. The contribution is a response-stability-guided algorithm for certified rollout selection under a declared conditional model. Numerical applicability is demonstrated; engineering-policy superiority, acceleration and continuous-domain convergence remain unestablished.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Surrogate-assisted engineering optimization spends expensive evaluations according to predictions and a model-management rule \citep{ref01,ref02,ref03,ref35}. Two-step planning must account for how the next design choice changes with the pending response. A complete response-dependent continuation partition can contain detail that is irrelevant to the current first-action decision.

We study a fixed first-action menu and an affine raw-response posterior-mean continuation. The proposed algorithm discovers local response regions in which a continuation is certified, encloses its positive terminal reward, and requests conditional posterior calculations only for encountered actions. An unassigned region retains a bounded continuation remainder. Its value uncertainty determines whether to discover another region or refine an existing integral. A checked decision inequality can certify the first action while some response identities remain uncomputed.

The methodological contribution has three connected parts: a regional terminal-reward enclosure; a lazy region-discovery and conditional-query algorithm with a positive-completion selection guarantee; and a finite-state implementation tested from clean engineering training inputs. A local error bound explains how unresolved switch cells and smooth integration contribute separately, without assuming a globally Lipschitz policy-composed value. Classical rollout, EI decomposition, affine dominance and interval arithmetic are acknowledged ingredients. Their individual use is not the novelty claimed here.

A positive log-response planning model and the existing raw-response mean continuation define different declared objects. This makes a positive compliance objective suitable for the stated terminal-reward range while retaining the original base policy. It does not establish improved statistical calibration. The lazy certificate validates queried bivariate marginals and bounds uncomputed positive conditional laws; it does not certify an unqueried full joint covariance or physical error.

Code and clean inputs were frozen before a single paired run on all 43 nontrivial archived beam-design states. Both the regional route and its uniform-reward control certify every state without historical action domains, old posteriors or integration certificates. Regional bounds reduce cell operations from 640 to 585 and conditional posterior constructions from 161 to 157, but the checked stage is slower. Existing engineering outcomes remain mixed. Earlier v2, certificate-transformation and adverse records are retained as supporting evidence; all states have already been observed in development.

\section{Relationship to existing lookahead methods}

Wu and Frazier develop practical two-step acquisition optimization, including one-dimensional Gauss--Hermite quadrature in the sequential setting \citep{ref39}. Two-step lookahead and one-dimensional conditioning are therefore established capabilities. Lee et al.\ reduce rollout computation through quasi-Monte Carlo, common random numbers and control variates, and also investigate policy search \citep{ref43}. Finite menus also appear in their policy-search formulation. Our additional computational object is a certified response-stability domain and its deterministic value-error bound for a fixed continuation, combined with posterior arithmetic and checked integration. The first-step baseline decomposition is also related to their use of EI as a known-mean control variate; the decomposition alone is not claimed as new. Here the stable-region remainder supplies a deterministic value range, rather than a variance-reduction result. No superiority over their acquisition optimizers is inferred.

Ling et al.\ develop an epsilon-optimal GP planning framework with Lipschitz rewards and an anytime branch-and-bound variant \citep{ref44}. Thus approximate-optimal GP planning itself is not the novelty claimed here. The present scope is a particular two-evaluation terminal objective, positive log-response model, affine raw-response continuation and finite binary-system error contract. A guarantee for this subproblem does not establish global or Bayes-optimal search. The event-error bound needs bounded rewards and certified policy identity on a response domain, rather than a global Lipschitz constant for the composed fixed-policy value. Curvature is used only on identified smooth action/incumbent pieces.

Affine posterior updates and breakpoint envelopes also occur in knowledge-gradient computation \citep{ref40,ref42}. Our earlier band-feasibility operator adapts that geometry to closed response intervals and independently replayable exclusions, using exact predicates \citep{ref41}. It serves as the complete-projection fallback. The local route does not require all those action domains initially.

Log-objective expected improvement is established \citep{ref45}; MPFR provides a correctly rounded arithmetic foundation \citep{ref46}. Neither positivity nor use of interval arithmetic alone constitutes the proposed increment. The concrete question is whether a response-stability certificate can guide a checked rollout solver to avoid unnecessary complete projections. The direct same-guarantee route is the relevant control for that question. Engineering-policy efficacy remains a separate outcome. The final lazy implementation removes historical action domains from its inputs and uses a minimal control that changes only the unknown-region reward range. Its specific computational object is a checked decision based on certified local identities and partial conditional-model information, rather than another claim of generic rollout priority.

\section{Discussion and limitations}

The final algorithm uses regional value uncertainty to choose computational work. Local action identity, a positive remaining-reward range and on-demand conditional marginals are sufficient to certify the finite-menu choice; complete global continuation projection is not an input requirement. The positive-completion guarantee remains valid across discontinuous fixed-policy rewards. This is a numerical optimization-selection method for a specific two-evaluation problem, not a new general acquisition principle.

The minimal paired comparison separates two effects. Avoiding complete projection is shared by both lazy routes. The regional reward range additionally reduces cell operations and conditional-posterior requests, but its extra marginal-moment work does not yield a time advantage in the recorded pass. The implementation repeats training solves and checks inside posterior requests; those costs are included. No further timing optimization or repeated measurement is used to change the conclusion.

The earlier v2 preparation used archived exact domains to select targets; its 114-to-25 projection count is therefore a reconstruction-stage result. The new run uses cleaned training snapshots and removes that dependency. Its separate 0-projection result is not assigned the earlier timing, full-joint covariance guarantee or 37.1\% certificate-width statistic. The three development stages answer different questions and their favorable numbers cannot be combined into a single performance claim.

The engineering scope remains one positive-compliance beam family with fixed parameters, a prescribed first menu and a raw-mean continuation. The finite numerical tolerance does not certify GP calibration, kernel-function error, PDE accuracy, continuous-domain convergence or real engineering regret. The lazy method validates used conditional marginals but not the entire unqueried covariance. A compatible full GP is included in the positive-law completion class, without asserting that every possible binary kernel construction admits one.

All 43 states were previously observed. Matching older selections is a consistency result, and the corresponding paid engineering outcomes remain mixed. The new method establishes executable, checked first-action optimization from clean training inputs; it does not establish better engineering designs, reduced physical evaluations or independent closed-loop validation.

The complete new records are saved. The missing older positive-response originals remain missing; the new implementations do not authenticate their timing or hashes. Three of 25 older paired-grid raw records remain unavailable. Their retained derived tables do not establish mesh convergence or physical accuracy at the decision margin. The preceding complete action-set manuscript, H1 obstruction, failed independent gate and all earlier unfavorable tables remain in the archive and supplement.

\section{Conclusion}

Response-stability-guided regional value enclosures support a lazy finite-menu rollout algorithm that requests conditional posteriors only for encountered continuations. Its selection certificate covers every positive completion of uncomputed conditional laws; a local error bound handles switching cells separately from smooth integration. Starting from cleaned training snapshots, both regional and uniform-reward routes certify all 43 engineering states without complete projection. Regional bounds use 585 rather than 640 cell operations and 157 rather than 161 conditional posterior constructions, but take more checked time. The contribution is certified computation of the declared engineering optimization subproblem. Mixed physical outcomes, observed-data reuse and the finite conditional-model contract limit stronger efficacy or generality claims.
