\section{Engineering design problem and numerical evaluation}\label{sec:lazy_results}

\subsection{Engineering decision and existing states}

The EngiBench Beams2D v1 workflow \citep{ref23} uses a symmetric MBB beam with $100\times50$ bilinear elements, SIMP exponent 3, stiffness limits $10^{-9}$ and 1, and Poisson ratio 0.3. Nineteen effective logits and an anchor define a $4\times5$ coarse density field interpolated to the mesh. A logistic shift fixes mean density at 0.35. Compliance is positive and smaller is preferable.

At an archived state, the engineering decision is which supplied design to evaluate first when a prescribed mean policy will choose the second evaluation after observing the response. The volume constraint is enforced by the density parameterization; the rollout calculation selects which feasible design receives the next compliance evaluation. Stored training responses and fixed surrogate parameters are used to certify this finite-menu comparison.

The 43 nontrivial states contain 20 menus with two first designs, 18 with three and five with four, giving 114 first-action branches. Every eligible continuation pool contains 1024 designs. Twenty-six states come from the archived-common-mean source and 17 from the source historically called previous holdout. Seventeen alias-only states remain in the 60-state metadata record. All states were observed during development, including those with the historical holdout label.

\subsection{Frozen execution and minimum control}

Each state supplies 100 stored training pairs. The kernel is a constant times an anisotropic Mat\'ern-$5/2$ kernel in 19 dimensions, with diagonal addition $10^{-6}$ and the recorded per-state hyperparameters held fixed. For the positive planning model, $c_0$ and $s_0$ are the binary64 mean and standard deviation of the stored log responses. Kernel evaluations define the binary matrices targeted by Equation~\eqref{eq:final_pair_posterior}. MPFR 4.2.1 uses 192-bit directed operations with outward binary64 endpoint export.

The implementation, protocol, four passing mathematical/program checks and 43 cleaned training snapshots were saved before execution at commit \texttt{8de86bbe1aeb1568bba754609cf10b8d093094c8}. The snapshots omit earlier action domains, nominal partitions, posteriors, decisions and future responses. One paired pass alternates method order. Both variants use the same local discovery, posterior arithmetic and reader, changing the unresolved-cell reward range. The producer cap is 2048 cell operations and 5 s per method/state, with reader time separately charged, and 240 s for the comparison. These settings remained fixed throughout execution.

Shared preparation costs 2.724387 s and reconstructs 43 training matrices, 114 augmented raw-selector systems and 43 initial first-marginal posterior enclosures. On-demand construction adds 318 bivariate posterior enclosures across both methods. The paired workflow therefore makes 361 validated positive-posterior construction calls. The current implementation repeats training solves and validation inside each call; the reported time includes that cost. Charging preparation to each method gives 13.451013 s and 14.950856 s, respectively, before input acquisition. The full paired workflow takes 25.840707 s.

Both methods certify all 43 states. The reader freshly checks the local domains, disjoint cell cover, conditional integrals and numerical selection. A subsequent record audit recomputes the declared tolerance and regret from the saved inputs. The computed decisions and per-file hashes were frozen before reading the earlier v2 choices or reference values. All 43 choices in each method match v2, all 228 branch/method intervals contain the earlier ordinary point-parameter references, and all queried conditional-parameter intervals overlap their earlier enclosures. These checks establish consistency with the earlier numerical record.
